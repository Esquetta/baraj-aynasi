How it works

  1. Every Sentinel-2 L2A scene of the three tiles over Istanbul's reservoirs from 2017 on is clipped to each reservoir, and clouds are masked with the scene classification layer.
  2. Water is mapped with NDWI, a ratio of the green and near-infrared bands: water reflects green light and absorbs near infrared. Water area is counted inside an outline drawn from each reservoir's high-water extent.
  3. For each reservoir, a curve from water area to occupancy is fitted on scenes up to June 2022 and tested on July 2022 to February 2024, the end of İBB's open daily data. The fit ignores scenes far off the curve and must rise with area.
  4. The 90% error bar comes from how far the curve misses on years it did not see. After February 2024, the frozen curves were scored once against İSKİ's reported figures.

Where it falls short

The project set itself one stop rule: if the error on the four largest reservoirs exceeded 10 occupancy points, publish the gap and stop. It was met for Terkos, Büyükçekmece and Darlık, so estimates are offered only for the reservoirs that passed.

Two kinds of trouble cause it. Shape: in a shallow lagoon like Büyükçekmece, area keeps shrinking while little water is left, and in a steep valley like Darlık area hardly changes at all. And late-spring scenes where algae turn the water's colour, so NDWI misses it.

A learned water mask

A small U-Net, trained on 237 reviewed image patches, replaces the NDWI threshold as an alternative. It keeps the water in algal scenes that NDWI drops, which halves Darlık's error (11.7 to 5.5 points). It does not fix Terkos or Büyükçekmece, whose problem is shape, and the estimates shown here still use NDWI.
